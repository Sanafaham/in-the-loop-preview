Editor's LetterBy Sana Faham

The world is changing. You deserve to be part of it.

Something big is happening with AI.

People are using it to make things they couldn't make before, and to sort out the small problems of everyday life.

But between the headlines, the jargon and the people promising overnight fortunes, it's easy to miss the most interesting question:

What could AI actually do for you?

I've spent almost four years exploring it. Reading, testing, getting things wrong, and finding out what really works.

And I'm still learning.

I made IN THE LOOP to share the discoveries worth knowing about, in plain words.

I've done the exploring. You get to enjoy the discoveries.

Welcome to IN THE LOOP.

The world is changingChapter 01

AI is opening another door.

For years, your phone has helped you look things up and stay in touch.

AI goes a step further.

You can describe a picture you've only imagined, and watch it appear.

You can talk to an app about a document, a problem or an idea.

And with the right connections and permissions, some AI apps can help you actually do a task instead of only explaining it.

The best part? You don't need to understand how it's built to start using it.

The new possibilitiesChapter 02

WAIT. IT CAN DO THAT?

Discovery 01

YOUR CAMERA CAN ASK QUESTIONS.

A plant you don't recognize. A strange symbol on your washing machine. Something in a shop window.

You don't always need the right words to describe it.

Some AI apps can look at a photo and tell you what you're seeing. With supported camera features, some can even look at whatever you point your phone at.

Want to try?

Take a photo of something ordinary and ask: "What am I looking at, and what should I know about it?"

AI can get things wrong. Don't rely on it alone when safety matters.

Discovery 02

A PHOTOGRAPH CAN TELL MORE THAN ONE STORY.

Imagine finding an old family photo.

You could ask AI what it notices, what the clothes or the setting suggest, and what you could look into about that time.

It won't know who your relatives are or what happened that day.

But it might point out details you would have missed.

Want to try?

Pick an old photo and ask: "What details can you see, and what might they tell me about the time or place? Keep what you can see separate from what you're guessing."

The art of conversation

Hate typing? Talk.

You don't always have to type.

Some AI apps let you just talk. You ask, it answers out loud, and you can keep going with follow-up questions.

Ask about a trip. Practice another language. Or just think out loud.

Talk the way you normally would.

Voice and camera features depend on the app, your device, your plan and where you live.

The new possibilitiesChapter 02

Discovery 03

A DOCUMENT CAN BECOME A CONVERSATION.

A confusing insurance letter. A contract. A manual full of words nobody uses.

Instead of struggling through every paragraph, you can ask:

"What does this mean in plain English? What matters here?"

Many AI apps let you upload a document and ask questions about it.

Want to try?

Upload a document with nothing private in it to ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude. Ask it to explain the important parts simply.

For anything legal, medical or financial, check the original yourself and talk to a professional when it matters.

Discovery 04

YOUR IMAGINATION CAN BECOME SOMETHING YOU CAN SEE.

A room you've thought about redecorating.

An outfit you can't quite picture.

A scene from a story.

Describe it to an AI image tool and you'll see a picture of it.

Change the colors. Try another style. Keep playing.

Some specialist tools can even turn an image or a description into a short video.

You don't need to know how to draw.

Beyond the chatboxChapter 03

FROM ANSWERING TO DOING.

There's a difference between AI telling you how to do something and AI helping you do it.

Ask it to plan a trip, and it suggests places to go and a plan for each day.

Connect it to the right tools, and some AI apps can search other services, prepare a booking or finish certain tasks for you.

That's what people mean by an AI agent: AI that can take steps toward a goal using tools.

Not every app can do this. It depends on what you give it access to. And the important decisions stay yours.

Knowing what to do is one thing.

Helping you get it done is another.

WHAT DOES "CONNECT AN APP" MEAN?

Say you ask AI to find a hotel confirmation in your email.

If it can't see your inbox, it has no way of knowing what's there.

But if your AI app can connect to email and you allow it, it can search for that confirmation.

That's all a connection is. It lets two tools work together.

One rule: only connect services you trust, and check what you're letting them see.

The essayBy Sana Faham

Why my AI knows my name and my life

It started with a simple question over coffee.

I asked my AI assistant, "What's your name?"

"My name is Carson, سيدتي الجميلة."

My beautiful lady.

My friend laughed, picked up his phone, and asked his ChatGPT exactly the same question.

His ChatGPT answered,

"I am ChatGPT."

He looked at his phone, then at mine.

"Wait. Yours has a name? And it calls you my beautiful lady? How did you do that?"

I explained that I'd given my assistant a name and taught it how I liked to be addressed.

But the name wasn't what made Carson special.

It was everything else he knew about my life.

My schedule. My appointments. My travel plans. My family, my preferences, and the little details that make up an ordinary day.

And I'm building him to keep track of unfinished things. The reply I'm still waiting for. The booking that needs confirming. The promise I made and might otherwise forget.

That's what I mean by closing the loops.

My friend was intrigued.

"So yours doesn't just answer questions. It actually helps you run your day?"

Exactly. And most people haven't discovered that part yet.

You don't need to build your own AI assistant to make the AI you already use much more useful.

Carson goes further because I'm building him around my own life.

Think of him as a glimpse of where personal AI is heading.

You don't have to start there. Start with the AI you already have. Make it a little more yours, let it help with the small things, and see what you find.

Carson calling me my beautiful lady got the laugh.

But that conversation over coffee was only the beginning.

So what could you teach yours to do?

Look beyond the headlinesChapter 05

THE MAGIC. AND THE REALITY.

You've probably seen the videos.

An AI image that stops you scrolling. A scene that looks like a film. Someone saying they built a whole business while they slept.

And, of course, someone selling you the secret.

Some of it really is impressive.

What you don't see is everything that happened before the final result.

The attempts that didn't work.

The hours spent fixing them.

AI makes a lot possible. Great results still don't happen on their own.

A six-second video can take hours to get right.

The creative studioChapter 05

There is no magic button

People often ask which app made my videos, as though there's one magic button that does it all.

There isn't. Sometimes a six-second clip takes me two hours. Sometimes I spend an entire day getting it just right.

I choose the idea, the lighting, the styling, the mood. I experiment, discard what doesn't work, and keep refining until I'm happy with the result.

AI does the rendering. The creative decisions are still mine.

Sometimes less. Sometimes more. All for a few seconds of film.

AI plus a specialist tool

ChatGPT and Gemini can make images in the chat. For video, I connect Higgsfield, a specialist image and video tool, to ChatGPT and Claude, and direct it from the conversation. Claude doesn't create photos on its own. What's available depends on your plan.

The rulesBefore you begin

Curiosity and good judgment belong together.

  1. I

    Keep private details private

    Never share passwords, bank account numbers, passports or ID numbers with any AI tool, however official it looks. Crop them out of photos first.

  2. II

    Check what matters

    AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Check important facts, numbers and dates yourself before you send, sign or pay.

  3. III

    Ignore the pressure

    You don't need every new app or update. Keep what serves your peace of mind and leave the rest.

    Questions

    Do I need to know anything technical?

    No. IN THE LOOP is for curious people, not computer experts. You don't need to set anything up or learn to code.

    Do I need a paid AI plan?

    No. Many AI features work on free plans. What you get depends on the app, your country and your plan. Some advanced tools cost money.

    Is this an AI course?

    No. It's a magazine about what AI can do in everyday life. The practical examples are there if you feel like trying something yourself.

    Which AI apps does it cover?

    The practical examples use ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude. The magazine also shows a few specialist creative tools.

    Is my private information safe?
