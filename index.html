ForewordFrom the editor

What if the assistant already on your phone could read the small print for you?

It can. It can also look at a photo of your fridge and suggest the week’s dinners. It can turn a lab report into plain words, so you walk into your appointment with better questions. It can draft the polite, firm letter you have been putting off.

Many people have asked an AI a question or two. Far fewer have handed it a real task from a real day and watched what happens.

This issue is my collection of those moments. The situation, the exact words to type, and the app you already have. No coding. No technical background. Just curiosity.

The EssayBy Sana Faham

Why my AI knows my name and my life

My assistant, asked “What is your name?”

“My name is Carson, Sayyidat al-Jamila.”

My beautiful lady.
My friend’s ChatGPT, same question

“I am ChatGPT.”

I was having coffee with a friend when I asked my assistant its name. Carson answered warmly. My friend asked his ChatGPT the same thing and got a plain reply. He looked at me, confused.

Anyone can give an AI a nickname. What he noticed was bigger. Carson knew my schedule, my appointments and my travel. He knew my family’s needs, my taste and my work. And he closes the loops. If something is left unfinished, he brings it back until it is done.

“How come yours runs your day,” my friend asked, “and mine feels like a search box?”

Here is the honest answer. Carson is a custom assistant I built for my own life, connected to my calendar and my tools. Your everyday assistant is simpler. But much of what surprised my friend is already in your hands.

Tell it who you are. Talk to it often. It starts to feel like yours.

What your app can do

Learn how you like answers. Remember useful details between chats when memory is on. With your permission, some apps can link to your email, files or calendar.

What a custom system adds

Something like Carson can watch my lists and follow up on its own. An everyday assistant mostly waits for you to ask. That is fine. Asking is where the discoveries start.

The RulesBefore you begin

Curiosity and good judgment belong together.

  1. I

    Keep private details private

    Never share passwords, bank account numbers, passports or ID numbers with any AI tool, however official it looks. Crop them out of photos first.

  2. II

    Check what matters

    AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Check important facts, numbers and dates yourself before you send, sign or pay.

  3. III

    Ignore the pressure

    You do not need every new app or update. Keep what serves your peace of mind and leave the rest.

    The Art of Conversation

    Hate typing? Talk.

    Talk to your assistant out loud, like a phone call. It answers in a natural voice.

    Tap the Voice icon in the message bar and start speaking. Free plans include limited voice time.

    Gemini

    Tap the Live icon to start a conversation. You can also show it what your camera sees.

    Claude

    Tap the sound wave icon next to the microphone, choose a voice, and speak. Included on the Free plan.
